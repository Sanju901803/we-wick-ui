👔 Ideal Payout Strategy (Salary vs Dividend)
  • Pay a modest salary first. A salary creates RRSP contribution room (18% of prior-year salary) and CPP pensionable earnings — dividends create neither. Many owner-operators pay themselves a salary around the CPP/EI thresholds or enough to maximize RRSP room, then top up with dividends.
  • Use dividends for the remainder. Dividends avoid CPP/EI premiums entirely and are taxed at preferential personal rates (dividend tax credit), but every dollar is taxed personally in the year it's paid — there's no ability to "smooth" income across years the way retained earnings can.
  • Don't pay out more than you need to spend. Any dividend/salary drawn now that you don't spend immediately gives up the tax-deferral advantage of leaving it in the corporation (see below). Keeping money corporate and only drawing what covers your personal budget is usually more tax-efficient.
  • Watch the passive income threshold. If the corporation's investment income exceeds $50,000/year, the small business tax rate deduction starts to shrink — this can push more corporate income into the higher general tax rate.
📈 Ideal Investment Strategy
  • Invest inside the corporation for tax deferral. Corporate tax rates on active business income (~12.2% small business rate) are much lower than personal rates, so leaving money in the corporation to invest lets more capital compound before it's eventually taxed on withdrawal.
  • Capital gains and RDTOH refunds soften investment tax. As modeled on the Contractor to FullTime tab, investment income triggers refundable tax (RDTOH) that comes back to you once a taxable dividend is paid — so the effective corporate tax rate on investment income is lower than it first appears.
  • Maximize personal registered accounts first. TFSA and RRSP room (the latter built from salary income) shelter growth from tax entirely or defer it — usually preferable to corporate investing for the same dollar, when you have room available.
  • Consider a holding company for larger portfolios. As retained earnings grow, a separate holdco can isolate investment assets from business liability and simplify succession/estate planning.
